# NUM for Business — the offering

**Updated:** 2026-07-24 · **Market:** Edinburgh · **Audience:** merchant-facing (sales conversations + the /business page)

---

## The one-line pitch

> **NUM fills the tables and seats you were about to lose.** Visitors ask our AI concierge what to do tonight; if you're a good fit, it books them. You pay only when it works.

Not "more customers." Every merchant has heard that. The specific promise is **perishable inventory** — a Tuesday 6pm table and three unsold seats on Thursday's tour are worth exactly nothing at closing time. NUM's job is to convert some of them.

---

## What a business actually gets

| | What it means to them |
|---|---|
| **Verified, high-intent guests** | People who asked "where should we eat tonight?" thirty seconds ago — not browsers |
| **Bookings to their phone** | One tap to accept or decline. No new system, no training, no logins to remember |
| **Control of their own words** | They see and edit the exact sentence NUM uses to describe them. That's the feature they care about most |
| **Guest demand insight** | Anonymised: "6 people asked about vegetarian options this week." Free market research they can act on |
| **Free to list** | No setup fee, no monthly minimum on entry. Pay only on outcomes |
| **Never misrepresented** | NUM cannot invent claims about them — it recommends only what's approved in their listing |

### What they *don't* get (say it plainly — it builds trust)
- Not a booking system replacement. NUM sits alongside whatever they use.
- Not guaranteed volume. It depends on visitor demand in their category.
- Not a review platform. Reviews inform ranking; NUM isn't TripAdvisor.

## Pricing

**Principle: they should never pay before NUM has paid off.** That's what gets a signature in week one from a business that's been burned by directory listings.

| Tier | Price | What's included | Who it's for |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Listed** | **Free** | Verified listing, appears in NUM's recommendations, bookings to phone, basic dashboard | Everyone — the default entry |
| **Commission** | **10% of booking value** *(or flat £2–4/booking for low-value covers)* | Everything in Listed. Charged only on completed bookings NUM sourced | The standard model |
| **Featured** | **£49/month** + reduced 7% commission | Priority placement in category, richer listing (photos/promos), demand insights, monthly performance review | Businesses seeing real volume who want more |

### Why this shape
- **Free entry** removes every objection in the first conversation. Supply depth is our constraint — don't put a price on the thing we need most.
- **Commission aligns us.** We earn when they earn. Easy to defend, easy to explain.
- **Featured is the upsell that arrives on its own.** A merchant doing 20 bookings/month asks for more visibility unprompted. Don't sell it on day one.

### Commission mechanics (be precise — this is where trust breaks)
- Charged on **completed** bookings only — no-shows and cancellations are free.
- Attribution: the booking must originate in a NUM conversation. Walk-ins who happened to hear of you elsewhere don't count.
- **Monthly settlement**, with a line-item statement showing every booking. Both sides see the same ledger (5arz PayRails).
- Disputes: flag any line within 30 days, we investigate against the conversation record.

## The two models we support

Both signed businesses are covered from day one — the dashboard adapts to which one they are.

### Hospitality — *booking-led*
Leads with **tonight's requests**. Guest, party size, time, any note ("window table", "anniversary"). Accept/decline in one tap. Dead-window filling is the pitch: *"tell us your quiet windows and NUM will steer people there."*

### Tours & activities — *capacity-led*
Leads with **upcoming departures and seats remaining**. NUM knows how many seats are left and can sell against them. Higher lead time, higher cancellation cost, so confirmations and reminders matter more. The pitch: *"three empty seats on Thursday is £120 you'll never get back."*

## The objections you'll actually hear

| They say | The honest answer |
|---|---|
| *"I already pay for OpenTable / Viator / TripAdvisor."* | Those are search — people already looking for you. NUM is recommendation — people who don't know you exist and are deciding right now. It's additive, and free to try. |
| *"How do I know the booking came from you?"* | Every booking is tied to a conversation we can show you. If it's ambiguous, you don't pay. |
| *"I don't have time for another app."* | There's no app. It's a link on your phone, and the only thing you must do is tap Accept. If it needs more than that from you, we've built it wrong. |
| *"What if the AI says something wrong about us?"* | It can only use the listing you approved, and you can edit it any time. It's not allowed to invent details — that's a hard rule in the system, not a policy promise. |
| *"What's the catch on free?"* | We only make money when you make money. If NUM sends you nothing, you pay nothing, and you can leave whenever. |

## The 30-minute onboarding

1. **Listing** — name, category, area, hours, contact channel they actually answer.
2. **Photos** — 3–5 real ones. Ask them to take a couple on their phone right there; borrowed stock photos are why listings feel dead.
3. **The description** — read NUM's draft sentence aloud to them. Their edit here is the highest-value input in the whole process.
4. **Dead windows** — which slots do they most want filled? This directly tunes recommendations.
5. **Commission** — agree it, record it.
6. **Dashboard on their phone** — have them accept a test booking while you watch. Add to home screen.
7. **Set the check-in** — one week out.

**Success signal at week 1:** they accepted a real booking without asking you how.

## What we measure per business

Visible to them in the dashboard, and to us in Control → Supply:

| Metric | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| Bookings sourced | The headline number they judge us on |
| Booking value + commission | What they earned vs what they paid |
| Accept rate | Low rate = wrong fit or bad timing; investigate |
| Response time | Slow accepts kill conversion — coach on it |
| Listing freshness | Stale photos → fewer recommendations |
| Guest asks | The demand they're *not* yet serving |
